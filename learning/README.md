# From quantum information to a recorded remote measurement

Alice and Bob can imitate the unread noise in this project by coordinating local Pauli kicks. Reproducing the weak detector's classical record **and** its quantum backaction is a different task. If the data can be replaced and saved after every short interaction, the required shared entanglement grows with the offered time resolution.

The single educational anchor is Mark M. Wilde, *Quantum Information Theory*, second edition, Cambridge University Press (2017), ISBN 9781107176164. The [source-to-project bridge](WILDE_BRIDGE.md) identifies the exact edition, verified section pointers and distance conventions. Primary research papers retain their separate scientific attribution in [related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md).

The route first fixes what the user can access and what the simulator must preserve. It then explains why a product input can expose a shared entangled resource, how a single joint truncation controls every causal test, and why rare event positions lead to a Poisson coefficient. The inefficient-record result follows only after its changed instrument is specified.
